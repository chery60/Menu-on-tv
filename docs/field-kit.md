# Field kit and the pilot neighbourhood (MT-13)

Written 20 October 2026. Density drives word of mouth now and the ad network later, so the pilot is one dense food street, not a city.

## Choosing the neighbourhood

Pick one area where:

- there are **60 or more counter-ordering outlets within 2 km** (cafés, quick service, bakeries, sweet shops, chaat and juice counters);
- most outlets have a queue that faces a wall where a TV could hang;
- a mix of owners, not one chain;
- the team can be there several times a week.

Walk it once with the outlet list below before deciding. Charan picks the area; write it here when chosen.

## The 60-second demo

Say it in the owner's language. Practise until it fits a minute.

1. **0 to 10 s.** "Namaste. How often do you change the prices on your board?" Listen.
2. **10 to 25 s.** Plug the stick into their TV (or hold up the demo phone's preview). "This is a menu on a TV. It was made from a photo of a paper menu."
3. **25 to 40 s.** On the phone, tap a dish on the Today tab. "Say the samosas run out. One tap, and every TV in the shop shows it sold out." Point at the TV as it changes.
4. **40 to 50 s.** "It works on the TV you already have, if it's new enough. I can check yours in five seconds." Open `/tv-check` on their TV if they agree.
5. **50 to 60 s.** "It's free for one screen. Can I come back tomorrow with your own menu on it?" Leave a card.

## When owners push back

| They say | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "My TV is old." | "Let's check it." Open `/tv-check`. If it fails: "A streaming stick fixes that; we can show you which one." |
| "No Wi-Fi in the shop." | "It needs internet to get changes, but it keeps playing your menu if the internet drops. A phone hotspot or a small data plan is enough." |
| "I don't have time to make it." | "Give me a photo of your menu. I'll make it and show you tomorrow." |
| "What does it cost?" | "One screen is free, with a small MenuOnTV mark in the corner. More screens are ₹499 a month each." |
| "Will it show ads?" | "Only if you say yes, never over your prices, and at most one slide in three. You keep half of what they pay." |
